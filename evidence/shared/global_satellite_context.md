# Global Satellite Election Context

As of: 2026-06-26

## Scope

This file supports non-U.S./non-Brazil satellite markets: Sweden, Quebec, Vancouver, Israel, and New Zealand.

The common theme is that these markets often separate party strength from government formation. In parliamentary systems, 'most seats' and 'next prime minister' are related but not identical.

## Parliamentary forecasting discipline

For parliamentary and MMP systems:
- Most-seats probability is not identical to prime-minister probability.
- Coalition compatibility matters.
- Threshold effects matter.
- Small parties can determine government formation even with modest vote shares.
- Incumbents can remain or lose office depending on bloc arithmetic, not just party plurality.

## Municipal/provincial discipline

For Vancouver and Quebec:
- Local issues, party brands, turnout, and candidate personal recognition matter more than national ideology.
- Polling may be sparse and volatile.
- Treat unsupported long-shot candidates as low probability.
- For municipal races, party labels may be weaker or more fluid than federal labels.

## Evidence hierarchy

Highest-value evidence:
1. recent high-quality polls;
2. election system and threshold rules;
3. candidate/party status and leadership changes;
4. coalition feasibility;
5. incumbency and approval;
6. campaign scandals or major endorsements.

Lower-value evidence:
- social media buzz without polling confirmation;
- prediction-market price, because the LLM prompt must remain blind;
- stale pre-2026 assumptions.

## Update checklist

Refresh this file when:
- new polls are released;
- major leaders resign or parties merge/split;
- coalition pledges change;
- election dates/rules change;
- a candidate is disqualified or formally nominated.
